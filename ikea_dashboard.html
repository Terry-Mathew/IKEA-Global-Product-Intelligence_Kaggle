Data Science Techniques Explained

TECHNIQUE 1 Distribution Analysis

What: Analyzing how data values are spread across a range.

Why: Reveals typical values, outliers, and data shape. IKEA prices are highly right-skewed (skewness: 5.57) with most products affordable.

TECHNIQUE 2 Correlation Analysis

What: Measuring statistical relationship between two variables (-1 to +1).

Why: Shows if variables move together. Price-Rating correlation is nearly zero (0.0099) - expensive does not mean better.

TECHNIQUE 3 Category Clustering

What: Grouping similar products to analyze patterns.

Why: Helps understand which product types perform similarly. Storage dominates with 50K+ products.

TECHNIQUE 4 Outlier Detection

What: Finding unusual data points using IQR (Interquartile Range) method.

Why: Identifies products with unusual pricing. Found 66,195 outliers (16.9% of dataset).

TECHNIQUE 5 Geographic Analysis

What: Comparing metrics across different countries/regions.

Why: Reveals market-specific patterns. Colombia/South Korea have highest average prices.

TECHNIQUE 6 Promotion Analysis

What: Comparing promoted vs non-promoted products.

Why: Measures marketing effectiveness. Promotions significantly boost engagement.

TECHNIQUE 7 Text Analysis

What: Word frequency analysis on product names.

Why: Reveals naming patterns. PAX, METOD, KALLAX dominate - modular systems strategy.

TECHNIQUE 8 Price Tier Segmentation

What: Segmenting products into price categories.

Why: Understands product portfolio. 37% of products are budget (under $50).

Strategic Recommendations

1

Immediate Quality Review: Fix or Discontinue 51 Products High Priority

Products like IVAR shelving (478 reviews, 2.8 stars), ROLFSTORP lighting (412 reviews, 2.7 stars), and ESKILSTUNA sofa (189 reviews, 2.7 stars) are generating significant negative feedback. Each has 100+ reviews - that is thousands of customers seeing poor ratings. Recommendation: Conduct quality review, improve design, or phase out.

2

Supply Chain Fix: Address Low Availability Markets High Priority

Egypt (32.5% unavailable), New Zealand (22.5%), Mexico (21.7%), Morocco (19.7%), and Jordan (18.4%) have significant inventory gaps. These are growth markets where supply issues could lose market share to competitors. Priority: Improve logistics and inventory forecasting.

3

Sofa Category Overhaul: Quality Improvement Initiative High Priority

Sofas and Armchairs has the lowest rating (4.23) - significantly below the 4.45 average. This is concerning as sofas are high-ticket items. Recommendation: Invest in quality control, improve materials, enhance warranty terms, and gather detailed customer feedback on specific issues.

4

Aggressive Promotion Strategy: Increase Marketing ROI Medium Priority

Data shows promoted products get 2.8x more reviews (452 vs 159). Promotional products also rate slightly higher (4.49 vs 4.45). Recommendation: Increase promotional budget, especially in underperforming categories. Use TOP_SELLER badges more strategically.

5

Market Expansion: Focus on Underserved Regions Medium Priority

Colombia (2,410 products), Chile (4,808), South Korea (5,324), and India (6,325) have significantly smaller catalogs compared to European markets (10,000+). These represent growth opportunities. Recommendation: Increase product assortment and localize offerings for these high-potential markets.

6

Leverage Success: Replicate Decoration Category Best Practices Low Priority

Decoration (4.62), Baby and Children (4.60), and Kitchenware (4.53) are the highest-rated categories. Study what makes these successful: quality materials, good value perception, functional design. Apply these principles to struggling categories like Sofas and Kitchen Appliances.

7

Value Marketing Campaign: Highlight Best Value Products Low Priority

Found 5,224 products with 4.5+ rating under $50. Products like TRATTKAKTUS cushion ($8.99, 5.0 stars) and BACKDRAG plant pot ($5.99, 5.0 stars) are hidden gems. Recommendation: Create value-focused marketing campaign showcasing these high-quality, affordable products.

8

Key Business Insight: Price Does Not Equal Quality Strategic

Correlation between price and rating is nearly zero (0.0099). This is powerful! It means customers do not perceive higher prices as better quality. Recommendation: Do not over-price assuming quality perception. Maintain competitive pricing while investing in actual quality improvements that will drive organic positive reviews.
